How to read this manual

This manual explains why modern NLP breaks when handling West African Kwa languages (Akan/Twi, Ewe, Ga, Fante), and how Leke uses orthographic normalization, byte-level fallback, and greedy subword merging to solve it.

Every principle follows a 4-part structure:

Limits

01 Orientation

The fragmentation crisis

In multilingual LLMs, tokenization is an economic and linguistic tax. A 10-word sentence in English might produce 12 tokens; the same 10 words in Twi or Ewe frequently produces 35 to 55 tokens in GPT-4 or NLLB-200.

The fertility gap

Fertility is the average number of subword tokens produced per word. Lower fertility means shorter context length, faster inference, and lower training compute costs.

02 Part I · Linguistics

Lookalike character harmonization

Keyboards in West Africa rarely feature native African Latin characters like ɛ (open e), ɔ (open o), ɣ (voiced velar fricative), or ɖ (voiced retroflex stop). Users substitute visually identical Greek or Cyrillic characters.

The lookalike collision

To a human eye, Greek ε (U+03B5) and African Latin ɛ (U+025B) look identical. To a tokenizer, they are completely different byte sequences:

Greek ε:        0xCE 0xB5
Latin ɛ (Kwa):  0xC9 0x9B

If an unnormalized model trains on text containing both, it allocates two separate vocabulary slots for every word containing that vowel (e.g. wɔfrɛ vs wɔfrε), diluting frequency and causing splits.

03 Part I · Linguistics

Phonemic tone & diacritics

In Kwa languages, tone is phonemic: changing the pitch accent alters the grammatical function or meaning of a word.

In Akan/Twi: pápá with high tones means "goodness", whereas papá means "father", and pàpà means "fan". Stripping tone marks removes vital semantic distinctions required for accurate translation and Text-to-Speech (TTS).

Leke's normalizer preserves tone by default, maintaining acute (´), grave (`), and circumflex (ˆ) marks. It provides an explicit flag strip_accents: true only for downstream tasks that demand tone-agnostic retrieval.

Byte-Level BPE vs Unigram

Which subword algorithm best models agglutinative morphology in African languages? Leke evaluated Byte-Level BPE against Unigram across all four languages on held-out test splits.

Why BPE won: Byte-Level BPE iteratively merges frequent adjacent character pairs into common syllable chunks (CV, CVC), matching Niger-Congo affixation. Unigram produced significantly higher word fragmentation rates (>2 subwords per word) on Ewe and Ga.

06 Part II · Algorithms

The 0.0% unknown-token guarantee

An out-of-vocabulary <unk> token destroys model generation. In machine translation, encountering <unk> drops entire names or critical nouns.

Leke implements Byte-Level BPE: the base vocabulary is initialized with all 256 individual byte values (0x00 through 0xFF). When an unseen symbol, foreign character, emoji, or mathematical notation appears, the tokenizer never emits <unk>; it seamlessly decomposes the character into its raw UTF-8 byte tokens.

07 Part II · Algorithms

Temperature upsampling & pooling

Akan/Twi and Ewe have millions of words in open corpora; Ga and Fante have far less. A naive merge of raw datasets would result in a vocabulary where 95% of tokens belong to Twi.

Leke uses temperature-based multinomial upsampling with $\alpha = 0.5$ during vocabulary training. Each language's sampling probability $p_i$ is smoothed according to its corpus size $N_i$:

p_i = (N_i)^α / Σ_j (N_j)^α

At $\alpha = 1.0$, sampling matches raw corpus size (high imbalance). At $\alpha = 0.0$, all languages are sampled equally (uniform). At $\alpha = 0.5$, low-resource Ga and Fante receive sufficient representation to achieve competitive 2.38 and 2.16 fertility rates without starving Twi.

08 Part III · Engineering

Rust core & PyO3 zero-copy

High-throughput data loaders in PyTorch cannot afford slow Python tokenizers. Leke executes all normalization and subword segmentation inside native Rust compiled with PyO3.

  • >356,000 tokens/second single-threaded encoding on standard x86_64 CPUs.
  • GIL Release: Batch encoding releases Python's Global Interpreter Lock (`Python::allow_threads`), allowing multiple PyTorch DataLoader workers to encode in parallel.
  • ABI3 Wheels: Ships precompiled CPython C-API stable ABI wheels, ensuring a single binary wheel installs cleanly across Python 3.9 through 3.13+.

10 Part IV · Case Studies

Seven things that broke, and what they teach

Real-world engineering lessons learned while building and benchmarking Leke.

#What brokeWhy it happenedHow it was solved
1 NFD vs NFC decomposition trap Combining diacritics in Ewe separated tone accents into distinct floating codepoints. Mandated Unicode NFC normalizer pass immediately before lookalike substitution.
2 Greek lookalike collision Online text used Greek ε and γ, producing duplicated vocabulary entries. Built externalized char_mappings.json mapping all lookalikes to Latin codepoints.
3 Ga vocabulary starvation Raw data pooling favored Akan 8-to-1, causing Ga words to split into individual letters. Applied temperature upsampling ($\alpha = 0.5$) during multi-language corpus ingestion.
4 Unigram excessive fragmentation Unigram pruning fragmented Ewe words over 64% of the time. Empirical benchmarking selected Byte-Level BPE as the production default.
5 Byte fallback sorting glitch Initial byte-level tokens were ordered incorrectly, corrupting round-trip decoding. Enforced standard byte-level BPE pre-tokenizer alphabet sort invariants.
6 Tone mark erasure Standard accent strippers erased Akan distinctions between pápá and papá. Made tone preservation the strict default; stripping is opt-in only.
7 Transformers JSON schema mismatch Custom normalizer was not recognized when loading via Hugging Face AutoTokenizer. Wrapped serialization into standard Hugging Face PreTrainedTokenizerFast bundles.

G Reference

Glossary

Key terms used throughout the Leke manual.

Fertility
The average number of subword tokens produced per input word (tokens ÷ words). Lower fertility indicates better compression and alignment.
Byte-Level BPE
Byte-Pair Encoding starting from 256 byte tokens. Guarantees 0.0% out-of-vocabulary rate on any input text.
Unicode NFC
Normalization Form Canonical Composition: combines base characters and diacritics into single precomposed codepoints (e.g. e + ´ → é).
Lookalike Character
A character from a foreign script (e.g. Greek ε) visually indistinguishable from an African Latin character (ɛ).
Temperature Upsampling
Exponentiating language dataset frequencies by a factor α (0 < α < 1) to amplify smaller languages during tokenizer training.
<unk> Token
Special unknown token emitted when encountering characters outside the model vocabulary. Guaranteed 0.0% in Leke.
